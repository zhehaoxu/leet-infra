# Notes: The Consistency Decoder's Two-Step Schedule

## Source

- HuggingFace diffusers v0.42.1, `src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_consistency_decoder.py`: line 13 marks `betas_for_alpha_bar` as "Copied from diffusers.schedulers.scheduling_ddpm.betas_for_alpha_bar"; its body spans 14-63 (cosine 40-41, laplace 45-48, exp 52-53, and `min(1 - ab(t2)/ab(t1), max_beta)` on 62). `ConsistencyDecoderScheduler.__init__` is on 100-119 (`sigmas = sqrt(1/ab - 1)` on 113, `sqrt_recip_alphas_cumprod` on 115, the three coefficient arrays on 117-119); `set_timesteps` on 121-134 (the hardcoded `[1008, 512]` on 129, and the rejection of any `num_inference_steps != 2` on 126); `init_noise_sigma` on 136-146; `scale_model_input` on 148-163; `step` on 165-212 (`x_0` on 196, `timestep_idx` on 198, the last-step test on 200-201, the re-noising on 204-207).
- The same `betas_for_alpha_bar` also lives in `scheduling_ddpm.py`; the three transforms here are exactly DDPM's, with `laplace` being the branch v0.42.1 added.

## Core Points and Pitfalls

- **`c_skip` / `c_in` carry \(\bar\alpha^{-1/2}\), not \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha}\).** Line 115 inverts `alphas_cumprod` before taking the square root, and the name `sqrt_recip_alphas_cumprod` is the hint. At \(t=1008\), \(\bar\alpha=5.21\times10^{-4}\), so the two spellings differ by \(1/\bar\alpha\approx 1919\times\) and `scaled_first` collapses by three orders of magnitude; at \(t=512\) the gap is only about \(2\times\), easy to dismiss as rounding when hand-checking a small \(T\). `check_three_transforms` pins this with an independently derived \(\sqrt{1+\sigma^2}\) - the identity \(\bar\alpha^{-1/2}=\sqrt{1+\sigma^2}\) holds, but the derivation route differs.
- **Re-noising uses the *next* timestep's coefficients.** Lines 204-207 read `self.sqrt_alphas_cumprod[self.timesteps[timestep_idx + 1]]`: locate the current step inside `self.timesteps`, then take the **following element's value** to index the length-\(T\) array. Hence \(x_{\text{second}}\)'s \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha}\) comes from \(t_1=512\) (0.701656), not from the current \(t_0=1008\) (0.022827). `check_renoise_uses_next_timestep` builds cases that distinguish the two numbers.
- **The last step does not re-noise.** The `if timestep_idx == len(self.timesteps) - 1: prev_sample = x_0` on lines 200-201 means `noise` enters exactly once, through \(x_{\text{second}}\); the only surviving path from `noise` to \(x_{\text{final}}\) goes via that intermediate. `check_last_step_is_plain_denoise` subtracts two calls (`f_second=0` with `noise=0` versus `noise=1`) and recovers the single channel \(c_{\text{skip}}[t_1]\cdot\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t_1}}\).
- **`timestep` is an array index, not a step counter.** Both `scale_model_input` (line 163) and `step` (line 196) index the length-\(T\) coefficient tables by the timestep **value**, which is why the statement takes \((1008, 512)\) as values and requires them inside \([0,T)\) in strictly decreasing order. Treating \(t_1\) as "the second step" and writing index 1 corrupts `scaled_second` and `x_final` at once.
- **`max_beta` only bites at the tail.** With cosine at \(T=1024\), `beta_0 = 4.026e-5` and the ceiling is only reached by `beta_1023`, whose raw value is \(1.0\) and gets clamped to \(0.999\). Laplace's betas are U-shaped (\(T=8\): 0.183, 0.063, 0.044, 0.034, 0.037, 0.055, 0.103, 0.945) and exp's are the constant \(e^{-12/T}\) (\(0.77687\) at \(T=8\)). `check_max_beta_clamp` measures all three.
- **A degenerate schedule must be caught.** With cosine and `max_beta=1.0` the final \(\beta\) is exactly 1, \(\bar\alpha\) reaches 0, and `1/ab - 1` on line 113 divides by zero (upstream yields `inf`; in plain Python this is a `ZeroDivisionError`), so this problem raises `ValueError` up front. Under the same arguments exp is safe, since its first \(\beta=e^{-12/T}\) stays far below 1.

## Deviations from Upstream

- Upstream freezes the defaults of `betas_for_alpha_bar(num_train_timesteps)` (`max_beta=0.999`, cosine) at construction time and `set_timesteps` rejects any \(N\neq2\). Here the transform name, `max_beta` and both timestep marks become arguments so each behaviour can be tested in isolation, while the "two steps" structure is kept.
- Upstream returns float32 betas from `betas_for_alpha_bar` and runs `cumprod` in float32; this problem requires float64 intermediates. Against a line-by-line transcription of the library, the worst relative deviation is \(2.3\times10^{-6}\) - exactly the magnitude of accumulated float32 rounding.
- Upstream draws its own noise with `randn_tensor` inside `step`; here `noise` is an explicit argument, so judging needs no RNG and no `generator`.
- `SchedulerMixin / ConfigMixin / register_to_config`, `ConsistencyDecoderSchedulerOutput` and the `return_dict` branch are all removed. The return becomes a 4-tuple that also exposes the two `scale_model_input` results, so a mismatch localises immediately.

## Division of Labor

- `diff-noise-schedule` drills the generic \(\beta/\bar\alpha/\sigma\) conversions. What is new here is `betas_for_alpha_bar`'s three transforms, the `max_beta` clamp, and indexing the coefficients by the timestep **value**.
- `diff-sigma-grid-construct` and `diff-rf-timestep-shift` re-lay a \(\sigma\) grid at inference time; the grid here comes from a training-time cumulative product, a different construction path.
- `diff-edm-preconditioning` uses the same \((c_{\text{skip}}, c_{\text{out}}, c_{\text{in}})\) names, but with EDM's \(\sigma_d/(\sigma^2+\sigma_d^2)\) form and an externally supplied \(\sigma\). The \(\bar\alpha^{-1/2}\) factor here is the DDPM-side inverse normalisation - the same naming, a different lineage.
- `diff-ddpm-step` performs the standard \(\epsilon\)-prediction posterior-mean update. Here the network emits \(x_0\) directly and the update is "jump to the next timestep and re-noise", which is closest to `diff-lcm-boundary-step`; the difference is that LCM still inverts an \(\epsilon\) prediction to obtain \(x_0\), whereas no noise prediction exists at all in this scheduler.
