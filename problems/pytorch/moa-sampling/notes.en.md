# Deep dive: Mixture-of-Samplers Distribution Blending (MoA)

## Origin
- **The parts that come from llama.cpp (verified against source; stated item by item)**: the two sub-conventions of step ① are taken straight from llama.cpp, confirmed in the `src/llama-sampler.cpp` text I fetched:
  - `llama_sampler_temp_impl`, when `temp <= 0.0f`, does **not** softmax: it finds the largest logit and writes `-INFINITY` into every other entry — exactly the "one-hot(argmax)" of the problem statement; on ties it keeps the first strictly-greater winner, i.e. the lowest index.
  - `llama_sampler_top_p_apply` cuts where the descending cumulative sum satisfies `cum_sum >= p`, taking the cut point at `i + 1`, i.e. **the element that first reaches \(p\) is still kept**, with `min_keep` guaranteeing a floor — the same convention as the collection's `top-p-sampling`, and the truncation rule used by each expert's nucleus here.
- **The part that is not llama.cpp mainline**: running several sampler chains in parallel, oversampling them and blending over the union is an **assembly technique** in the llama.cpp ecosystem (the community calls it MoA / mixture-of-adaptive-samplers), not a resident library stage: in current master, `common/sampling.cpp`'s `common_sampler_type_to_str` returns only `dry / top_k / typ_p / top_p / top_n_sigma / min_p / temperature / xtc / infill / penalties / adaptive_p` — **there is no moa** — and grepping `src/llama-sampler.cpp` finds no `*moa*` implementation either. So this problem is described and judged as "an implementation technique whose math is fixed by the starter", without claiming a line-for-line upstream source.
- **The two "MoA"s are not the same thing — do not cite the wrong one.** The bank item for this problem reads "llama.cpp moa / 2406.04692". Checked: arXiv:2406.04692 is Wang et al., *Mixture-of-Agents Enhances Large Language Model Capabilities* (2024, ICLR 2025), whose MoA is a **layered scheme where several LLMs read each other's text outputs and an aggregator continues** — it lives in the **text domain**, needs extra forward passes and concatenation, and its math has nothing to do with "a weighted sum of \(E\) next-token distributions at one position". It is therefore **not a basis for the problem statement**, and only shares the letters. The paper behind the actual procedure named in the starter ("Mixture-of-**Adaptive**-samplers") — its arXiv id could **not** be verified under the restricted network available here: **pending source verification (to be sourced)**. Note additionally that the id 2401.10096, which appeared in the authoring brief, resolves to *Long time regularity for 3d gravity waves with vorticity* (Ginsberg, 2024), a mathematical-physics paper unrelated to this problem, and **must not be cited**.

## Full derivation and index conventions
Let \(E\) be the number of experts and \(V\) the vocabulary size; \(e\) indexes experts, \(t\) indexes token positions (\(0\le t<V\)), always **0-based**.

1. **Per-expert distribution.**
   \[
   p^{(e)}_t=\begin{cases}\mathbb{1}\!\left[t=\arg\max_{t'} \text{logits}_{e,t'}\right], & \tau_e\le 0\\[3pt]
   \dfrac{\exp(\text{logits}_{e,t}/\tau_e)}{\sum_{t'}\exp(\text{logits}_{e,t'}/\tau_e)}, & \tau_e>0\end{cases}
   \]
   Let \(\pi_e\) be the descending ordering of \(p^{(e)}\) (ties by ascending index) and \(c^{(e)}_j=\sum_{i\le j}\pi_e[i]\). The nucleus is \(K_e=\{\pi_e[0],\dots,\pi_e[j_e-1]\}\) with \(j_e=\min\{j: c^{(e)}_j\ge p_e\}\) and \(j_e\ge 1\) (with \(p_e=1\) the nucleus is the whole vocabulary). \(q_e\) is \(p^{(e)}\) divided by \(c^{(e)}_{j_e-1}\) on \(K_e\), zeroed outside it, and put **back into the original index order**.
2. **Oversampling and the joint candidate set.** Expert \(e\)'s shot \(j\) is
   \[
   s_{e,j}=\min\Big\{t:\ \sum_{t'\le t} q_e(t')\ \ge\ u^{\text{exp}}_{e,j}\Big\},
   \]
   where the sum runs along the CDF in **original index order \(0,1,\dots,V-1\)**, not in probability order; positions outside the nucleus carry mass \(0\) so the CDF stalls there, hence \(s_{e,j}\in K_e\).
   \(\mathcal U=\operatorname{sort}\big(\{s_{e,j}: 1\le e\le E,\ 1\le j\le m\}\big)\), with \(|\mathcal U|\le E\cdot m\) (in the worked example \(Em=4\) shots yield only \(3\) candidates, because both shots of expert 0 land on token 0).
3. **Renormalize over \(\mathcal U\), then blend.** With \(D_e=\sum_{t\in\mathcal U} q_e(t)\), \(r_e(t)=q_e(t)/D_e\) for \(t\in\mathcal U\); experts with \(D_e=0\) are skipped entirely.
   \[
   p_{\text{mix}}(t)=\sum_{e:\,D_e>0}\frac{w_e}{\sum_{e'}w_{e'}}\,r_e(t),\qquad
   \text{filtered}(t)=\begin{cases}p_{\text{mix}}(t)/\sum_{t'\in\mathcal U}p_{\text{mix}}(t'), & t\in\mathcal U\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}
   \]
   The order matters: **normalize each expert over \(\mathcal U\) first, then weight** — not "weight first, then restrict to \(\mathcal U\)". On the example data: \(D_0=0.755271\) (token 1 is inside expert 0's nucleus but outside \(\mathcal U\), so \(1-0.755271=0.244729\) of expert 0's mass is excluded), \(r_0=[0.880797,0.119203,0.0]\); \(D_1=0.817862\), \(r_1=[0.0,0.777300,0.222700]\); \(\text{filtered}\big|_{\mathcal U}=[0.616558,0.316632,0.066810]\).
4. **Final sample** uses the same inverse-CDF convention: \(\text{chosen}=\min\{t:\sum_{t'\le t}\text{filtered}(t')\ge u\}\), falling back to \(V-1\) if nothing satisfies it. In the example the CDF is \([0.616558,0.616558,0.933190,1.0,1.0]\) and \(u=0.5\) hits at the very first term → \(0\).

## Core traps
1. **The blend happens in probability space and after truncation.** Each chain's truncation (temperature + top-\(p\)) completes on its own, and what is blended is the **truncated, renormalized** \(q_e\); "average the \(E\) logits and then softmax" is a different operator altogether (logit-space averaging = a geometric-mean type blend in probability space) and will never reproduce the statement's numbers.
2. **"Renormalize over \(\mathcal U\) first" is neither skippable nor movable to the end.** The experts' mass on \(\mathcal U\) differs (\(0.755271\) vs \(0.817862\) in the example); without this step, a wider-nucleus expert would waste its weight.
3. **The last normalization barely moves the numbers when \(m\ge1\)**: every \(r_e\) already integrates to \(1\) over \(\mathcal U\) and \(\sum_e w_e/\sum w = 1\), so \(p_{\text{mix}}\)'s total mass is \(1\) by construction. Over 400 randomly generated configurations (greedy \(\tau=0\) experts included, \(E\le3\), \(V\le9\)) I measured that total mass to lie in \([0.9999998808,\,1.0000001192]\) — pure float32 rounding, so the step is **defensive** (the real trigger would be an expert with zero mass on \(\mathcal U\), which cannot happen for \(m\ge1\), since each expert has at least one shot inside \(\mathcal U\), hence always \(D_e>0\)). Defensive does not mean removable: drop it and any upstream change (allowing \(m=0\), or negative weights) silently leaves an unnormalized distribution.
4. **A zero-weight expert still enters \(\mathcal U\)** — oversampling happens before weighting. Measured: with `weights=[0,1]` and one shot per expert, \(\mathcal U=\{0,3\}\) and the final `filtered=[0.017986, 0, 0, 0.982014]`, i.e. "the surviving expert renormalized over \(\mathcal U\)"; token 0 only carries \(0.017986\) because the **zero-weight** expert dragged it into the candidate set.
5. **A \(\tau_e\le0\) expert is "one vote, nailed down"**: its one-hot CDF is \(0\) before argmax and \(1\) after, so all \(m\) shots hit the same token (example: \(\mathcal U=\{1\}\), and `filtered` is exactly one-hot). So when a greedy route is mixed with divergent routes, its \(m\) shots really contribute only \(1\) candidate — it enters \(\mathcal U\) differently from a stochastic route.
6. **Inverse CDF always uses original index order.** The zeros outside \(\mathcal U\) create plateaus in the CDF, and "first index with cumulative \(\ge u\)" naturally skips them: in the example \(u=0.7\) lands on token 2 and \(u=0.99\) on token 3. An implementation that "sorts, picks by rank" gets tokens 2 and 3 the wrong way round.
7. **Do not use `torch.multinomial`**: it carries its own RNG state, is not guaranteed to equal the explicit-\(u\) inverse CDF (nor its tie behaviour), and the collection's convention is to hand-compute the CDF.
8. **Degeneracies the judge does not cover**: with \(m=0\), \(\mathcal U=\varnothing\), and the reference solution returns an all-zero `filtered` plus the fallback index \(V-1\) (measured); the statement already requires \(m\ge1\).

## Versus neighbouring problems
- **vs `dynatemp-sampling`**: dynatemp has a single distribution source; it interpolates the normalized entropy \(C\) into a temperature window and still ends with one `softmax(logits/dyn)` — **it rescales the temperature only, it never composes distribution sources**. Here \(E\) routes run in parallel with \(E\) temperatures and \(E\) top-\(p\) values, and what is composed is the distribution itself; there is no "global temperature" quantity at all.
- **vs `top-p-sampling` / `top-k-sampling` / `min-p-sampling`**: those are **truncation** operators (the candidate set can only shrink, survivors are renormalized within the same single distribution). Top-\(p\) here is just an internal step of each route; the overall operator is a **blend**: the candidate set is the **union** of the routes' draws (it may contain tokens outside some route's nucleus, and may gain tokens some route never considered), and the final distribution is not a monotone deformation of any single \(q_e\).
- **vs `adaptive-p-sampling` / `mirostat-v2-sampling`**: those two are stateful closed-loop controllers (EMA target / threshold \(\mu\) fed back step by step), with state in both arguments and return values. This problem is **stateless** and resolves in one shot.
- **vs `penalty-sampling` / `logit-bias-sampling`**: both of those reshape **one** distribution source in the **logit** domain (penalties derived from history counts / a static bias map supplied by the caller); this problem merges **several** distribution sources in the **probability** domain and never adds anything to the logits.
- **vs `llama-sampling-pipeline` (in flight in the same batch)**: that problem assembles the **ordering** inside a **single** chain — bias→penalties→dry→top-k→top-p→min-p→xtc→temp — emitting the survivor set stage by stage. This problem is **\(E\) chains in parallel, then merged**, and only examines the "oversample → union → weighted blend over the union → single sample" segment; no pipeline assembly.
- **vs the MoE problems (`moe-topk-gating` etc.)**: there the weights come from a router network and what is blended is the experts' **output representations** (usually top-k sparse); here the weights are an explicit argument and what is blended is **\(E\) next-token distributions at the same position**. Similar name, different layer.

## Cross-check design
The reference solution takes the tensor path (`softmax` + `argsort(stable=True)` + `cumsum` prefix scan + per-expert masked accumulation). The judge's `_oracle` goes down a completely separate path: plain Python lists, per-expert hand-rolled `math.exp` softmax (denominator via `math.fsum`), descending order via `sorted(range(v), key=lambda i: (-p[i], i))`, the prefix found by **element-wise accumulation** until first \(\ge p_e\), and inverse CDF by an `enumerate` accumulation loop — no tensor sorting or indexing anywhere. Besides the example and 25 random full cross-checks (with greedy \(\tau\le0\) experts mixed in), there are invariant cases: \(|\mathcal U|\le Em\), strictly \(0\) outside \(\mathcal U\), `filtered` summing to \(1\), `chosen` \(\in\mathcal U\); plus three degeneracy assertions: \(E=m=1\) ⇒ one-hot, a \(w=0\) expert only affects \(\mathcal U\), a \(\tau=0\) expert repeats the same index across shots. All compared with `allclose(atol=1e-5, rtol=1e-5)`.

## Complexity
Per chain: softmax \(O(V)\), top-\(p\) sort \(O(V\log V)\), total \(O(EV\log V)\); oversampling is \(E\cdot m\) inverse-CDF draws at \(O(V)\) each; the blend accumulates \(E\) contributions per surviving token, \(O(E\,|\mathcal U|)\le O(E^2 m)\); the final sample is \(O(V)\). Space \(O(EV)\). In real deployments \(E\) is "how many models / chains", typically \(2\sim4\), so the quadratic-in-\(E\) term is negligible and the bottleneck remains the per-route \(V\log V\) sort.

## Practical notes
Oversampling replaces "who deserves a chance" from a **threshold** (how much mass to keep) with a **draw** (which tokens the \(m\) shots hit), and the price is that the candidate set now depends on the random numbers, its variance shrinking with \(m\); at \(m=1\) the blended result is largely decided by each route's luck. Tuning \(m\) is not equivalent to tuning \(w\) — a route with \(w_e=0\) still changes the candidate set (see trap 4), so "switching a route off" is not expressible in these semantics; the only way is to make its mass vanish too. Weights need not be pre-normalized (the implementation divides by \(\sum w\) explicitly), but their sum must be positive.
