# In Depth: Sophia-II Clipped Second-Order Update

## Source

- The algorithm: Liu et al., *Sophia: A Scalable Stochastic Second-order Optimizer for Language Model Pre-training*, arXiv:**2305.14342** (verified; some registration materials write 2305.14373, which is not this paper). This problem implements the paper's Algorithm 3, the **Sophia-H** variant: the diagonal Hessian is estimated by Hutchinson-type random probes and folded into an EMA. The judge environment is offline and has no copy of the paper, so **the algorithm is defined by the problem statement and starter comments**: the denominator is the first power \(\max\{H,\varepsilon\}\), the clip is element-wise to \([-\rho,\rho]\), and \(\hat H\) is an explicit argument.
- Library side: kozistr/pytorch_optimizer ships a `Sophia` (mostly as `SophiaG`/`SophiaH`; exact file and attribute naming **to be re-sourced**). Real implementations compute \(\hat H\) inside `step` via Hutchinson probes; for CPU determinism this problem **promotes \(\hat H\) to an explicit argument**, and the estimation cadence is controlled by the judge's feeding sequence — mathematically equivalent to the paper's \(k\)-step reuse.
- One-line summary: **Sophia = Adam with \(\sqrt{v}\) swapped for curvature \(H\), and "adaptive shrinking" swapped for a hard clip**. The more curvature information the denominator has, the more the clip is a backstop rather than the main event; early in training, while \(H\) has not warmed up, the clip is effectively the main mechanism.

## Full derivation and index convention

Let \(t\) be the parameter's step (the state counter starts at 1). The division of labor among the three quantities is the key to this problem:

- **\(m_t\) (gradient EMA, β₁)** decides the **direction**. It updates every step, regardless of whether a Hessian estimate arrived.
- **\(H_t\) (curvature EMA, β₂)** decides the **step size**. It updates only on "estimate steps": \(H_t=\beta_2H_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\hat H^t\); on non-estimate steps \(H_t=H_{t-1}\) (**frozen** — not zeroed, not skipped). The paper estimates every \(k\) steps; this problem encodes that switch as `hessians is None` — a judge sequence may space estimate steps arbitrarily (the tests include a 20-step sequence estimating every 3rd step).
- **the clip (\(\rho\))** decides the **trust region**. Each component of the direction \(u_t=m_t/\max\{H_t,\varepsilon\}\) is clamped to \([-\rho,\rho]\). Since \(\varepsilon\) only floors the denominator, the **displacement bound** comes entirely from the clip: without weight decay \(|\Delta\theta_t|=\text{lr}\cdot|u|\le\text{lr}\cdot\rho\) always holds (the tests verify the aggregate bound over a 10-step sequence).

Two-operator vs one-operator style: the reference solution writes \(u=(m/h_{\text{clamp}}).\mathrm{clip}(-\rho,\rho)\) (division and clamping as **two separate ops**); the independent oracle computes \(u=m/\mathrm{clamp}(h,\varepsilon)\) first and clamps second, with all fresh tensors. The two are bit-identical (same two float ops in the same order), but **fusing division and clip into one compound expression**, or clipping the denominator before dividing, is wrong — the latter wrongly transfers the bounding job to the denominator.

Why \(\sqrt{H}\) is wrong: the preconditioner \(m/H\) responds to curvature **inversely to the first power** — double the curvature, half the step. Writing \(m/\sqrt{H}\) (Adam's muscle memory) makes a doubled \(\hat H\) scale the displacement by \(1/\sqrt2\approx0.707\) instead. There is a dedicated assertion (\(\beta_1=\beta_2\) fixed, a single gradient step, \(\hat H_2=2\hat H_1\), \(\rho\) huge so the clip never binds): the ratio of the two displacements must be **exactly 2**. That turns "first power" from prose into a checkable fact.

On the EMA starting from zero: \(H_0=0\), so \(H_t=(1-\beta_2)\sum\beta_2^{t-s}\hat H^s\) is an average **with a deficit factor** (early \(H\) is \((1-\beta_2)\) times \(\hat H\), further shrunk by the \(\beta_2\) tail). With β₂=0.99, step 1 sees \(H_1=0.01\hat H\) — the quotient explodes and gets almost fully clamped by \(\rho\). This is not "the first steps are broken"; it is the intended use of the clip: **the trust region backstops the early phase when the curvature estimate is not yet trustworthy**. It also explains why the SGD degeneration must take \(\beta_2=0\) (then \(\hat H\equiv1\) enters \(H\) directly).

## Key points and common mistakes

1. **First power, not square root.** The statement and two dedicated assertions (doubling halves; SGD degeneration) all pin it. The classic error is carrying Adam's `sqrt(v)` habit over as `m / h.sqrt().clamp_min(eps)`.
2. **The bounding lives in the clip, not the denominator.** Raising \(\varepsilon\) to "prevent explosions" is wrong: \(\varepsilon\) only raises the denominator's floor (with \(H=0\) the quotient is \(m/\varepsilon\), usually larger); the real \(|\Delta\theta|\le\text{lr}\cdot\rho\) comes from the clip. The tests include a step with \(\hat H\equiv0\): the displacement must be **exactly** \(\text{lr}\cdot\rho\), directed against the sign of \(m\).
3. **Alignment and placeholders for `hessians`.** `hessians` matches **all parameters** (flattened across groups) in length and order; positions of parameters with `grad is None` still carry a tensor (the judge passes zeros). Implementations that zip group-local parameters without a global index silently misalign under multiple groups. The reference builds a global index `{id(p): i}`.
4. **\(H\) frozen ≠ parameter skipped.** A `hessians=None` step freezes only \(H\): \(m\) EMA's as usual and \(\theta\) still moves by \(-\text{lr}\cdot\mathrm{clip}(m/\max\{H,\varepsilon\},\rho)\). Implementing "no \(\hat H\) ⇒ skip the whole step" stalls parameters on non-estimate steps — the opposite of the paper's \(k\)-step reuse; the tests assert "after one estimate, two pure-gradient steps keep descending".
5. **Decoupled weight decay goes first**: \(\theta\leftarrow\theta(1-\text{lr}\cdot\lambda)\) before anything, and \(m\) uses the **raw** \(g\) (same convention as opt-adamw). Decay never enters the \(m\)/\(H\) statistics.
6. **All-zero state, no bias correction.** Sophia does not divide by \(1-\beta^t\): the deficit of \(H\) (too small early) is backstopped by the clip, and \(m\)'s early deficit is small by design — that is the algorithm's tradeoff. Do not "helpfully" add bias correction; the judged trajectory diverges immediately.
7. **In-place updates**: write back through `p.mul_/add_/addcdiv_` or `copy_`; the return value only forwards `closure`. Rebinding `p = ...` to a fresh tensor silently loses the update (the parameter list still holds the old object).
8. **float32 and tolerances**: element-wise comparisons use `allclose(atol=1e-5, rtol=1e-5)`. Clip boundaries (exactly \(\pm\rho\)) are float-sensitive — divide then clamp; do not merge the two clamps into one `clamp(h, eps, 1/ρ)`.

## Differences from neighboring problems

- **vs `opt-adamw`**: same skeleton (decoupled decay, two EMAs, bias correction...), two forks: ① the denominator \(\sqrt{v}\) is computed from \(g^2\) locally, while Sophia uses \(\max\{H,\varepsilon\}\) — first-power curvature fed from outside; ② Adam has no clip (\(\sqrt{v}\) naturally bounds the step), Sophia has an element-wise \(\pm\rho\) clip. So the Adam intuition "double the gradient ⇒ double the step" is broken by the clip here. Sophia also **skips** the \(1-\beta^t\) bias correction.
- **vs `opt-copt` (C-Optimizer)**: both clip to \(\pm\rho\) and both subclass Optimizer, but they clip different things — copt's clip acts on the **Adam-style direction** \(u\), preceded by a continuous cosine gate that usually shrinks it; Sophia's clip acts on the **curvature-preconditioned** \(m/H\) with no direction gate. Hence copt's \(|\Delta\theta|\le\text{lr}\cdot\rho\) is rarely attained, while early Sophia hugs the bound — "does the displacement equal lr·ρ routinely?" separates the two trajectories.
- **vs `opt-galore` (GaLore)**: both consume extra second-order/structural input, but GaLore's \(P\) is an **orthonormal basis of a gradient subspace** (project, run Adam, map back) and the judge feeds a matrix factor; Sophia's \(\hat H\) is **per-coordinate curvature** (element-wise preconditioning, no subspace change). One changes the subspace the update lives in, the other changes per-coordinate step sizes.
- **vs `opt-lookahead`**: Lookahead's freeze/rollback is a weight-level periodic behavior, Sophia's \(H\)-freeze is a state-level behavior tied to the estimation cadence; they are orthogonal and composable — the former writes \(\theta\) every \(k\) steps, the latter writes \(H\).

## Cross-check convention and complexity

- Main criterion: the independent recompute \(\_oracle\_sophia\) — no Optimizer class, pure torch element-wise per Algorithm 3 (m/H in fresh tensors, division and clamping as two steps), deliberately ordered differently from the reference solution; a 20-step sequence with sparse estimate steps (every 3rd) is compared step by step with `allclose(atol=1e-5, rtol=1e-5)`.
- Hard reference: with \(\beta_1=\beta_2=0\), \(\hat H\equiv1\), \(\rho=10^{12}\), wd=0 the trajectory matches **native `torch.optim.SGD`** bit-for-bit at \(10^{-7}\).
- Structural assertions: doubling \(\hat H\) ⇒ displacement ratio exactly 2 (not \(2^{-1/2}\)); \(\hat H\equiv0\) ⇒ displacement exactly \(\text{lr}\cdot\rho\) against m's sign; without decay the total displacement over 10 steps is ≤ 10·lr·ρ (1e-5 slack for float32); a `grad is None` parameter is bit-identical afterwards.
- Complexity: \(O(\text{numel})\) per parameter per step (one EMA, one division, one clamp); estimate steps add one \(\hat H\) EMA, same order. Extra memory is one \(H\) tensor the size of the parameter — same price as Adam's \(v\).
